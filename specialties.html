What We Treat

Whatever You’re Facing, You Don’t Have to Face It Alone

Our therapists have supported many people through anxiety, trauma, depression, ADHD, relationship difficulties, substance use, grief, and other deeply personal experiences.

There is nothing shameful about needing help. You will not be judged or expected to explain everything perfectly. Our clinicians understand how difficult these experiences can be and provide a private space where you can speak honestly about what is happening.

The right care can help you understand your symptoms, develop healthier ways of coping, improve your relationships, and feel more in control of your life. Everyone’s progress is different, but meaningful change is possible.

Support for What You’re Experiencing

Select a topic to learn what it can look like and how treatment may help.

Anxiety can feel like constant worrying, overthinking, expecting the worst, or being unable to relax. You may appear successful and capable while privately feeling overwhelmed.

It can also cause physical symptoms such as a racing heart, tightness in the chest, nausea, dizziness, shaking, or difficulty breathing. Panic attacks may appear suddenly and feel frightening even when there is no immediate danger.

Treatment can help you understand what drives your anxiety, calm your body’s stress response, reduce avoidance, and develop practical ways to manage fearful thoughts.

We can help with: General anxiety • Panic attacks • Health anxiety • Social anxiety • High-functioning anxiety • Perfectionism • Chronic worry • People-pleasing • Fears and avoidance

Trauma may come from one overwhelming event or from repeated experiences such as emotional neglect, instability, abuse, loss, chronic stress, medical experiences, or unsafe relationships.

Its effects may include unwanted memories, nightmares, shame, distrust, emotional numbness, strong reactions to reminders, or feeling constantly prepared for danger.

Our trauma-informed clinicians begin by helping you develop safety and emotional regulation. Difficult experiences are explored gradually and at a pace that respects your readiness. You will not be forced to discuss everything immediately.

We can help with: PTSD • Complex trauma • Childhood trauma • Attachment wounds • Relationship trauma • Medical trauma • Emotional abuse • Hypervigilance • Emotional numbness

Depression does not always look like sadness. It may feel like numbness, irritability, exhaustion, isolation, hopelessness, or going through the motions while continuing to meet everyone else’s expectations.

You may lose interest in people or activities that once mattered. Basic tasks can begin to require more energy than you have.

Treatment can help you understand the emotional, relational, biological, and situational factors affecting your mood. Therapy can support you in rebuilding structure, connection, motivation, and meaning. Psychiatric care may also be considered when appropriate.

We can help with: Depression • High-functioning depression • Emotional numbness • Low motivation • Burnout • Isolation • Hopelessness • Low self-worth • Co-occurring anxiety

ADHD affects more than attention. You may struggle to begin or complete tasks, estimate time, remember responsibilities, stay organized, or understand why knowing what to do does not make doing it easier.

ADHD can also involve restlessness, impulsive decisions, emotional intensity, sensitivity to criticism or rejection, and becoming overwhelmed easily. Years of being misunderstood may lead to anxiety, shame, or low confidence.

Our therapists and psychiatric providers can help you understand how your brain works, strengthen executive-function skills, develop practical systems, manage emotions, and explore medication when appropriate.

We can help with: Adult ADHD • Child and adolescent ADHD • Executive-function challenges • Procrastination • Time management • Organization • Emotional intensity • Rejection sensitivity • Medication evaluation and management

Many couples are not lacking love—they are caught in patterns neither partner knows how to change. Repeated arguments, emotional distance, broken trust, or difficulty communicating can gradually weaken the relationship.

Couples therapy helps both partners understand the pattern beneath the conflict, express their needs more clearly, repair emotional injuries, and decide how they want to move forward.

Therapy is not about deciding who is right. It is about helping both people recognize what is happening between them and respond differently.

We can help with: Communication • Recurring conflict • Emotional disconnection • Trust • Infidelity recovery • Intimacy • Premarital concerns • Parenting conflict • Separation and discernment

Grief can follow a death, divorce, diagnosis, caregiving experience, relocation, estrangement, or the loss of a future you expected to have.

There is no correct timeline for grief. You may feel sadness, anger, guilt, relief, numbness, or several emotions at once. Healing does not require forgetting what happened or forcing yourself to “move on.”

Therapy offers space to honor what was lost, understand how the experience has changed you, and gradually make room for meaning and life again.

We can help with: Bereavement • Complicated grief • Divorce • Anticipatory grief • Caregiver loss • Ambiguous loss • Identity changes • Relocation • Major life transitions

Manipulation, gaslighting, control, criticism, and emotional invalidation can leave you questioning your memories, instincts, and worth. Even after the relationship ends, fear, guilt, confusion, and unhealthy emotional attachments may remain.

You do not need to prove that another person has a particular diagnosis before seeking support. Therapy focuses on what you experienced and how it has affected your life.

Treatment can help you understand harmful patterns, restore self-trust, strengthen boundaries, process trauma, and reconnect with who you are outside the relationship.

We can help with: Narcissistic abuse • Emotional abuse • Gaslighting • Trauma bonds • Coercive control • Boundary recovery • Self-doubt • High-conflict relationships • Post-separation recovery

You do not have to reach “rock bottom” to deserve help. Substance use is often connected to trauma, stress, shame, relationships, loneliness, or emotional pain—not simply a lack of willpower.

Support is available whether you are questioning your substance use, working toward sobriety, returning after a relapse, or protecting your long-term recovery.

Treatment can help you understand triggers, develop healthier coping strategies, reduce the risk of relapse, repair relationships, and address mental health concerns connected to substance use.

We can help with: Alcohol and drug concerns • Early recovery • Relapse prevention • Recovery planning • Co-occurring mental health concerns • Family support • Long-term recovery

Struggles with eating and body image are rarely only about food or appearance. They may be connected to shame, trauma, perfectionism, identity, control, comparison, or a persistent feeling of not being good enough.

These concerns are not always visible. You do not need to look a particular way or meet a specific diagnosis to deserve support.

Our outpatient therapy can help you understand the emotions and beliefs connected to eating and body image, reduce shame, improve emotional regulation, and build a more compassionate relationship with yourself. When more specialized or intensive care is needed, we can help identify appropriate resources.

We can help with: Body dissatisfaction • Disordered eating • Restrictive patterns • Emotional eating • Binge eating • Food-related anxiety • Shame • Perfectionism • Eating-disorder recovery support

You deserve therapy where you do not have to educate your provider, minimize your experiences, or leave part of yourself outside the room.

LGBTQIA+ people may seek therapy for anxiety, depression, trauma, grief, relationship concerns, or any other part of life. Some may also experience rejection, discrimination, identity questions, family conflict, or pressure to hide who they are.

Affirming care means that your identity is respected and is not treated as a problem to correct. Therapy can help you strengthen self-acceptance, navigate relationships, establish boundaries, process painful experiences, and live more authentically.

We can help with: Identity exploration • Coming out • Gender and sexuality • Minority stress • Family rejection • Relationships • Shame • Belonging • Self-acceptance

More Than One Concern? That’s Normal.

Anxiety may be connected to trauma. Depression may occur alongside ADHD. Relationship difficulties may affect substance use, grief, burnout, or self-worth. Real life rarely fits neatly into one category.

You do not need to decide which concern comes first. SHIFT offers therapy, psychiatric care, and recovery support so your treatment can reflect the complete picture. With your permission, providers may collaborate when coordinated care would benefit you.
